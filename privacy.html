Privacy policy

Effective 2 October 2026. Applies to the Oulak iPhone app and this website.

The short version

  • You sign in with Apple. The app asks for your email address, and you can hide it with Apple's private relay. The app never shows it.
  • Your location is rounded to about 1 km. Other people see only your city, area and the distance between you.
  • A letter is hidden from the recipient until the bird lands.
  • No ads, no tracking, no third party analytics, and I do not sell data.
  • You can delete your account inside the app. That deletes your data and ends the link between Oulak and your Apple ID.

Who is responsible

Oulak is made by Baturhan Cihan, an individual developer. I decide how the data described here is used. You can reach me at baturhancihan@gmail.com.

What Oulak collects and why

Sign in with Apple
You can only sign in with Apple. The app asks Apple for your email address (not your name). Apple lets you share your real address or hide it behind a private relay address. The email, real or relay, is kept by the sign in service and is not shown in the app or to other users. Apple may also leave the email out, and the app works without it. Your account is identified by an internal ID.
Username
You choose a username when you start. Other people use it to find you and to send you letters, so treat it as public.
Approximate location
With your permission, while you use the app, Oulak reads your location to measure how far a bird must fly. The server rounds it to a grid of about 0.01 degrees (about 1 km) and stores that rounded point with your city, area name and country code. Only you can read the stored point. Other people see only your city, area and the distance in whole kilometres. The server also keeps the times you updated your location, so that it can limit changes to three in 24 hours.
Letters
To deliver a letter, Oulak stores its text and details: who sent it, who receives it, which bird carries it, the flight times, the origin and destination city, the distance, and whether it was read. The sender can see the text at any time. The recipient can see it only after the bird has landed. Letters are text only.
Bird names and letter style
The names you give your birds and your choices of paper, envelope and seal are stored in your profile. Other people you write to see them on your letters.
Push notifications
If you allow notifications, the app registers a device token with Oulak, together with the platform, the environment (test or production), the app version and your device language. The server uses these to ask Apple to deliver notifications such as a bird leaving, landing or getting home. A notification never contains the text of a letter. The server also keeps a queue of the notifications for each letter, so that they go out at the right time. During testing the server also keeps a delivery log of notifications it sends: the device token and the notification text (for example who sent you a bird, never the text of a letter). It is used to check that notifications work.
On your device
The app keeps a few settings on your phone. They are not sent anywhere.

Apple refresh token

When you sign in, the app sends Apple's authorization code to a server function, which exchanges it for a refresh token and stores it in a table that the app and other users cannot read. The only purpose is to let me revoke the Sign in with Apple link when you delete your account. It is not used for anything else.

Who processes the data

I do not sell your data and I do not share it for advertising. These providers process it to run the app:

  • Supabase hosts the database, sign in service and server functions. The project runs in the London region (eu-west-2).
  • Apple provides Sign in with Apple and delivers push notifications. Apple's own privacy policy applies to what Apple handles.

The app has no advertising, no tracking, and no third party analytics or crash reporting.

How long data is kept

  • Account, profile, location, letters and bird settings are kept until you delete your account. Letters do not expire by themselves.
  • A push token is removed when you sign out of the app, or when Apple reports that the token is no longer valid.
  • The Apple refresh token is kept until you delete your account.
  • The testing delivery log of notifications (device token and notification text) is for checking that notifications work. It is not tied to your account in the database, so I clear it by hand rather than automatically, and it is not removed by account deletion itself.
  • A scheduled job moves flights through their stages every 10 seconds, and another deletes the database's own job run history after one day. Neither touches your letters.

Deleting your account

Open the app, go to Profile, then Privacy, then Delete account. When you confirm, Oulak:

  • asks Apple to revoke the Sign in with Apple link;
  • deletes your account, profile, stored location, bird names and style, device tokens and queued notifications;
  • deletes every letter you sent and every letter you received, including birds still in flight.

Because a letter belongs to a conversation between two people, the other person loses those letters too. If Apple cannot be reached, the deletion still goes ahead, but then you may need to remove Oulak yourself in your Apple ID settings under Sign in with Apple. Deletion cannot be undone.

The hosting provider may keep technical backups for a limited time, which are outside what I can change day to day.

Your choices

  • You can turn location access off in iOS Settings. Without a location Oulak cannot work out flights.
  • You can turn notifications off in iOS Settings.
  • You can hide your email with Apple's private relay when you sign in.
  • To ask what is stored about you, or to ask for a correction, write to me. Where the law gives you more rights, I will respect them as far as I can.

Children

Oulak is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect data from them. The app has no age check. If you believe a child has an account, write to me and I will delete it.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections. Access rules in the database let each person read only their own data and the letters they are allowed to see. Letters are stored as readable text, so as the person who runs the service I can technically access the database. I do not read letters. No system is perfectly secure, and I cannot promise otherwise.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, I will update the date at the top and tell you in the app or on this page.
